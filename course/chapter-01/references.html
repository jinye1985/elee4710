02 / EQUATIONS & IDEALIZATIONS

Models and checks

Unless explicitly attributed to the original lecture, parameter values and slider ranges are illustrative choices. The checks below document equations and numerical consistency; they do not establish the behavior of physical hardware.

Ideal square-wave H-bridge

MIT-INV CV-ST-HBRIDGE
vout=+Vdc for 0≤phase<0.5; vout=−Vdc for 0.5≤phase<1.

Parameters. Vdc: 12–96 V; default 48 V. Phase repeats over one electrical cycle.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Purely resistive load; ideal switches; square-wave operation.
  • Q1 is upper left, Q2 lower left, Q3 upper right, Q4 lower right. Q1+Q4 is positive; Q3+Q2 is negative.
  • Same-leg pairs are never active together. Real gate timing and dead time are required but are not simulated.
  • Current-path dashes indicate conventional current direction, not electron speed.

Checks

  • At phase 0.25 and 48 V, vout=+48 V. At phase 0.75, vout=−48 V.
  • The output is labeled square-wave AC, not sinusoidal filtered AC.

Ideal diode bridge

CV-MIT-RECTIFIERS ADI-BRIDGE
vin=Vpeak sin(2π phase); vout=|vin|.

Parameters. Vpeak: 12–96 V; default 48 V.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Four ideal diodes; zero source impedance; resistive load; no smoothing capacitor.
  • Alternating diode pairs keep the same load polarity; output is pulsating DC.
  • A wire jump marks the AC/DC wire crossing with no connection.

Checks

  • At phase 0.25, vin=48 V and vout=48 V. At phase 0.75, vin=−48 V and vout=48 V.
  • At zero crossings, vout=0 and no current path is highlighted.

Ideal averaged buck converter

MIT-BUCK
Vout,avg=D Vin; switch-node voltage is Vin during on-time and 0 during off-time.

Parameters. Vin=48 V; duty ratio D=0.20–0.80, default 0.50.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Periodic steady state, continuous inductor current and sufficiently large L and C.
  • The on-state connects input to the inductor; the off-state uses the freewheel diode.
  • Output is an averaged model; ripple, startup, discontinuous current and component sizing are not calculated.

Checks

  • D=0.20 → 9.6 V; D=0.50 → 24 V; D=0.80 → 38.4 V.

Resistive-load AC voltage controller

CV-PU-ACAC CUP-ACAC
Let θ=2π phase and α be firing angle in radians. Pass Vpeak sin θ when θ mod π ≥ α; otherwise output 0. Vrms=(Vpeak/√2)√[1−α/π+sin(2α)/(2π)].

Parameters. Vpeak=48 V; α=0–150°, default 60°.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Ideal antiparallel thyristors and purely resistive load; each conducting device turns off at current zero.
  • Same supply-cycle repetition; RMS control is not frequency conversion.
  • Only one example of the broader AC–AC category.

Checks

  • At α=0°, Vrms≈33.941 V. At α=90°, Vrms=24 V.
  • Independent midpoint numerical integration at α=0°,30°,60°,90°,120°,150° agreed with the analytic RMS to better than 0.000001 V.

Resistive transmission equivalent

OS-POWER OS-TR
I=Pload/Vreceive; Ploss=I²R; Vsend=Vreceive+IR; Psend=Pload+Ploss.

Parameters. Pload=100 kW; total R=2 Ω; Vreceive=1–20 kV, default 10 kV.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Single-phase, unity-power-factor resistive equivalent; voltage and current are RMS values. Transformers are ideal; reactive effects and generation losses are excluded.
  • Voltage is the receiving-end RMS voltage of the single-phase equivalent; the sending value includes the resistive drop.
  • At constant useful power and R, loss varies with the inverse square of receiving voltage.

Checks

  • At 1 kV: I=100 A, Ploss=20 kW, Vsend=1.2 kV.
  • At 10 kV: I=10 A, Ploss=0.20 kW, Vsend=10.02 kV.
  • At 20 kV: I=5 A, Ploss=0.05 kW, Vsend=20.01 kV.
  • Vsend×I=Pload+Ploss for every displayed input.

Instantaneous local power balance

EIA-ENERGY OS-POWER DOE-SMART
Pgrid=Pload−Psolar−Pbattery.

Parameters. Load=20–100 kW (default 60); solar=0–80 kW (default 35); battery=+20 kW discharge, 0 standby, or −20 kW charge.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Lossless instantaneous balance, not an energy-duration simulation.
  • Positive grid power means import; negative means export.
  • No battery capacity, state of charge, inverter limits, voltage or network constraints.

Checks

  • Default: grid=60−35−0=25 kW import.
  • Load 20, solar 80, battery +20: grid=−80 kW (export).
  • Load 100, solar 0, battery −20: grid=120 kW (import).

μ=NIA n; τ=μ×B.

Parameters. Uniform B directed right; left current out of page and right current into page.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Left conductor force points up and right force points down, giving clockwise torque.

Ideal rotating-coil generator

OS-GEN
Φ=BA cos θ per turn; ε=NBA(2πf) sin θ.

Parameters. N=100; B=0.50 T; A=0.020 m²; f=0.5–2 Hz, default 1 Hz.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Uniform field, planar coil, constant rotational frequency, open-circuit ideal EMF.
  • θ is the coil-normal-to-field angle, not the angle between the field and the coil plane.
  • Animation runs at one quarter of the stated physical cycle rate for inspection.

Checks

  • At f=1 Hz, EMF peak=2π V≈6.283 V.
  • At θ=0°, flux per turn=0.010 Wb and EMF=0. At θ=90°, flux=0 and EMF is positive peak.
  • Doubling f doubles EMF peak; flux amplitude stays fixed.

Ideal transformer at constant power

OS-TR
r=Ns/Np; Vs=r Vp; Ip=P/Vp; Is=P/Vs; Rload=Vs²/P.

Parameters. Vp=120 Vrms; Np=100; f=60 Hz; P=240 W. r=0.5,1,2,3.

Assumptions and meaning

  • Ideal lossless transformer; both windings share common changing flux.
  • Load resistance changes with ratio to hold output power at 240 W.
  • No saturation, leakage, copper loss or core loss; winding strokes are symbolic.
  • Physical frequency is 60 Hz on both sides; flux animation is slowed to 0.3 Hz.

Checks

  • Ratios 0.5,1,2,3 give Ns=50,100,200,300 and Vs=60,120,240,360 Vrms.
  • Ip=2 Arms; Is=4,2,1,0.666667 Arms.
  • Rload=15,60,240,540 Ω; VpIp=VsIs=240 W.
