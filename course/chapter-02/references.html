Worked-example assumptions

These assumptions apply to the supplied steady-current exercises. The time-varying inductor demonstration adds a prescribed sinusoidal current and a lossless quasistatic model; its parameters are recorded below.

  • Steady-current / magnetostatic lumped magnetic-circuit model.
  • Core relative permeability is held constant at the exercise-given value; calculated high B does not validate that approximation for a real unspecified material.
  • Uniform B across each segment area and H approximately uniform along each segment mean path.
  • Leakage flux neglected, and all relevant turns link the modeled flux.
  • Fringing neglected; effective air-gap area equals adjacent core face area.
  • Core path lengths exclude air gaps unless lc is explicitly given as mean core length (Example 1.4).
  • All calculations use SI units and unrounded intermediate quantities; source final answers often use rounded intermediates.

SECTION 01

Field direction and Ampere’s law

C01

Conventional current fixes magnetic-field orientation through the right-hand rule. Reversing current reverses the field.

∮ H·dl = Σ signed Nₖiₖ

Magnetostatic / magnetoquasistatic model. Animated marks indicate field direction, not moving magnetic particles.

L01 pp.3–7 M01 pp.4–5
C02

For a uniform mean path, the loop equation reduces to Hl=Ni; for a toroid H depends on radius.

H ≈ Ni/l; H(r) = Ni/(2πr)

A mean-path rectangular-core approximation is different from an exact full-field solution.

L01 pp.5–7 M01
SECTION 02

H, B, flux and linkage

C03

H is magnetic field intensity, B is flux density, Φ is magnetic flux and λ is flux linkage.

B = μH; μ = μ₀μᵣ; Φ = ∫ B·dA; λ = NΦ

B=μH uses a linear constitutive model. λ=NΦ assumes the same flux links every turn.

L01 p.8 M01 M06 Eq.13.1
C04

For uniform normal flux, Φ=BA. The quantities have different physical units and must not be conflated.

H: A/m; B: T; Φ: Wb; λ: Wb-turn; μ: H/m; 1 cm² = (10⁻² m)² = 10⁻⁴ m²

Use Φ=BA only for the stated uniform-field approximation. Apply the square to the centimeter conversion factor as well as the length unit.

L01 p.8 M06 N03
C05

The course calculation value of vacuum permeability is an excellent instructional approximation.

μ₀ ≈ 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m

The approximation sign avoids treating this as an exact modern SI defining constant.

L01 p.8 M11
SECTION 03

MMF, reluctance and circuit analogy

C06

A winding supplies MMF Ni; a uniform linear segment has reluctance l/(μA).

ℱ = Ni; ℜ = l/(μA); Φ = ℱ/ℜ

Reluctance is not an electrical resistance and its presence does not itself imply Joule loss.

L01 pp.11–12 M02
C07

Series magnetic sections share flux; MMF drops add. Branch fluxes obey continuity at a junction.

Σ ℱ = Σ Φⱼℜⱼ; Σ signed Φⱼ = 0

No leakage is included in the lumped circuit. Use the correct topology before summing reluctances.

L01 pp.15–17,27–29 M01 M09
SECTION 04

Air gaps and MMF allocation

C08

Core and gap reluctances add along one series flux path; their MMF drops can differ greatly.

ℜc = lc/(μ₀μᵣAc); ℜg = g/(μ₀Ag); Ni = Hc lc + Hg g

The core’s μᵣ must not be inserted into the air-gap reluctance.

L01 p.15,pp.18–24 M02 M09
C09

Fringing spreads the field near a gap. Equal gap and core face areas are a simplifying approximation.

Φ = BcAc = BgAg

The simulator uses Ag=Ac and neglects fringing/leakage. Any fringing drawing is qualitative, not a finite-element result.

L01 p.15 M02 pp.3–4
C10

In a mixed core-gap path, dividing total Ni by each separate segment length gives the wrong local field.

Hⱼ = Bⱼ/μⱼ; ΣHⱼlⱼ = Ni

Ampere’s law remains valid; it is the full-loop balance, not an instruction to assign all MMF to every segment.

L02 p.2 M01 M09
SECTION 05

Geometry and unequal cross sections

C11

Reluctance depends on mean path length and the area normal to flux, including core depth.

A = depth × limb thickness; ℜtotal = Σlⱼ/(μⱼAⱼ)

Mean core lengths exclude gap length. Example 1.4 directly supplies core length and must not have its gap subtracted again.

L01 pp.18–26 L02 p.1
C12

The same series flux can produce different flux densities in sections with different areas.

B₁ = Φ/A₁; B₂ = Φ/A₂

In Example 1.2 the horizontal yokes have area 0.010 m²; the vertical limbs have area 0.015 m². Both are core, not air-gap sections.

SECTION 06

Branched magnetic networks

C13

In the interactive flux divider, common-limb flux divides between two return branches with equal branch MMF drop.

Φ = Φ₁ + Φ₂; Φ₁ℜ₁ = Φ₂ℜ₂; ℜeq = ℜc + ℜ₁ℜ₂/(ℜ₁+ℜ₂)

This is an illustrative one-source network, distinct from the supplied two-source Example 1.3. Branch reluctances include their return yokes.

M01 M02
C14

The lower-reluctance parallel path carries more flux. The divider follows from continuity and loop balance.

Φ₁ = Φℜ₂/(ℜ₁+ℜ₂); Φ₂ = Φℜ₁/(ℜ₁+ℜ₂)

The supplied reluctances are positive, constant teaching values. There is no inferred cross section or measured material from the schematic geometry.

M01 M02
C26

The supplied two-source Example 1.3 instead combines two loop fluxes through a shared gapped center limb.

Φg=Φ₁+Φ₂; (ℜo+ℜm)Φ₁+ℜmΦ₂=ℱ₁; ℜmΦ₁+(ℜo+ℜm)Φ₂=ℱ₂

Here ℜm includes center-core plus gap reluctances. Corrected source arithmetic is recorded in the exercise audit.

SECTION 07

Inductance, voltage and energy

C15

A single winding in a fixed linear common-flux model has L=N²/ℜ.

λ = NΦ = Li; L = N²/ℜ

L describes the winding/core system. It is not a separate property of the gap alone.

L01 pp.30–32 M02 M07
C16

Induced emf opposes a change in linked flux; passive terminal voltage uses the corresponding positive derivative convention.

ℰ = −dλ/dt; v = dλ/dt = L di/dt for constant L

Different sign conventions must not be mixed. Fixed DC current with fixed geometry does not produce a Faraday emf.

M06 M07 M02
C17

A linear inductor stores magnetic energy; in a gapped high-permeability structure a large share can be in the gap.

W = ½Li²; u = B²/(2μ) = BH/2; gap energy share = ℜg/(ℜc+ℜg) for equal-area series segments

These formulas assume a reversible linear model. The gap-share expression follows by integrating energy density over each segment volume; it is 6/7≈85.7% for the stated inductor. With p=vi=dW/dt, negative power returns energy and a full ideal cycle has zero net transfer.

M08 M04 M12
C27

For a nonlinear linkage curve, a local derivative generally differs from the linkage-to-current ratio.

Ldiff = dλ/di; Lsec = λ/i

This local distinction assumes a specified linkage-current curve or material state. It is not a complete model of arbitrary hysteresis history.

M13
SECTION 08

Two coupled series coils

C18

The two supplied series coils can aid or oppose according to their winding/current orientation.

ℱ = (Na ± Nb)i; Φ = (Na ± Nb)i/ℜ

The series current is common. A signed flux-linkage convention is required for terminal inductance.

L01 pp.33–35 L02 p.1
C19

Self-inductance and mutual inductance separate the effects of each coil’s current.

La = Na²/ℜ; Lb = Nb²/ℜ; |M| = NaNb/ℜ; Lseries = La+Lb ± 2M = (Na ± Nb)²/ℜ

These formulas use a linear, fully linked common-flux model. With both current references into the dots, λa=Laia+Mib and λb=Mia+Lbib. The course’s NaΦ/i under simultaneous excitation is a linkage/current ratio, not La in isolation.

SECTION 09

Real magnetic materials

C20

Ferromagnetic materials are nonlinear and history-dependent; fixed μᵣ cannot be extrapolated through saturation.

Secant permeability B/H differs from incremental permeability dB/dH.

The source’s 2.5 T and 3.9 T outputs follow ideal exercise assumptions, not a verified material curve.

L01 pp.13–14 M03 M05
C21

Remanence and coercivity describe a hysteresis loop; loop area represents cycle loss per unit volume.

wcycle = ∮ H dB

Any analytic curve in the lesson is explicitly schematic and is not fitted manufacturer data.

M03 M05
C22

Thin insulated laminations constrain circulating currents and reduce eddy-current heating; this mechanism differs from hysteresis.

Changing magnetic flux induces currents; electrical resistance dissipates their energy.

Sheet planes run parallel to the main flux. Current paths are schematic; no quantitative efficiency or core-loss estimate is displayed.

M03 M06 M09
C23

Real material specifications must identify test conditions rather than a universal saturation number.

Example only: N87 data depend on H, frequency and temperature.

The linked TDK datasheet is an external evidence example. It is not the material used by the interactive models.
