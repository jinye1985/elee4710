C07-M1 Balanced rotating MMF

Id
C07-M1
Title
Balanced rotating MMF
Formula
Fk=cos(theta−sequence*alpha_k)[cos(alpha_k),sin(alpha_k)]; F=sum(Fk).
Parameters
Phase axes 0, +120, −120 degrees; normalized phase current peak 1.
Assumptions
Two-pole electrical cross section; balanced sinusoidal currents; no saturation, harmonics or slot effects.
Checks
720 angle/sequence combinations: norm(F)=1.5; Fx=1.5 cos(theta); Fy=sequence*1.5 sin(theta).

C07-M4 Exact complex per-phase circuit and torque

Id
C07-M4
Title
Exact complex per-phase circuit and torque
Formula
Ymag=1/Rc−j/Xm; Y2=1/(R2/s+jX2); E=V/[1+(R1+jX1)(Ymag+Y2)]; I2=EY2; Pag=3|I2|²R2/s; Te=Pag/omega_s.
Parameters
Illustrative 460 V line-to-line, Y, 60 Hz, four poles; R1=.35, X1=.7, X2=.7, Xm=20, Rc=300 ohm; R2=.10–.80 ohm.
Assumptions
Balanced RMS steady-state linear model; fixed frequency; rotor quantities stator-referred; parameter values selected for teaching, not a named machine; no saturation, skin effect or heating; load=100 N m is illustrative.
Checks
Checked signed slips −.25 through 1.4, including exactly zero and one: Pin=Pag+Pcu1+Pcore, Pag=Pcu2+Pconv, Te*omega_m=Pconv; Pcu2 nonnegative. At s=0 evaluate open rotor branch directly.

Id
C07-M5
Title
Total three-phase power accounting
Formula
Pin=Pag+Pcu1+Pcore; Pcu2=sPag; Pconv=(1−s)Pag; Pshaft=Pconv−Pfw; eta=Pshaft/Pin.
Parameters
Pag=5–25 kW; s=.01–.30; illustrative Pcu1=450 W, Pcore=300 W, Pfw=750 W.
Assumptions
Independent specified losses demonstrate accounting only; not a physical efficiency map or a fit to Example 2.
Checks
Input equals all losses plus shaft output; rotor loss plus converted power equals air-gap power; limits are nonnegative throughout the control domain.

Source clarifications and corrections

Lecture p. 8 implicitly divides line voltage by sqrt(3) without specifying the stator connection. The lesson declares Y and the approximation E1≈Vphase, and gives the alternative delta result.
Lecture p. 11 mixes a three-phase flow diagram with equations written without the factor of 3. This chapter consistently uses total three-phase powers with RMS phase currents.
Lecture p. 12 writes eta=1−s under simplified losses. The lesson identifies 1−s as rotor conversion fraction and states the loss assumptions needed for it to equal total efficiency.
Lecture Example 2 asks for torque, but p. 15 stops after rotor copper loss. Both electromagnetic and shaft torque are completed and distinguished.
MIT Chapter 10 equation 55 displays Pout=Pag−Pw although adjacent equations and its MATLAB listing subtract rotor copper loss before shaft losses. The internally consistent relation Pshaft=(1−s)Pag−Pfw is used and numerically checked.
All lesson motor cutaways, circuits and canvases are newly authored explanatory renderings of cited machine principles. No measured characteristic or external photo is fabricated or implied. No optional video was required for coverage.
